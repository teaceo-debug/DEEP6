# Setup #2: Multiple Nodes

---

## Overview

A High Volume Node (HVN) is the price level within a single footprint bar where the most contracts traded. When two or more consecutive footprint bars share an HVN at the exact same price level, that's a Multiple Node. The software highlights these automatically in yellow.

Multiple Nodes mark price levels where institutions repeatedly chose to transact. That repetition signals intent. When price returns to that level, the same institutional logic applies: position holders defend, opposing side closes. The result is a sharp reaction away from the node level.

This setup does NOT require Bid x Ask data. It works on Forex futures and any instrument with volume.

---

## Logic Behind the Setup

A single HVN at a price level could be coincidence. Two or more consecutive HVNs at the same level is a pattern. Institutions were repeatedly drawn to that exact price across multiple bars. They built positions there, or they defended a level there, or both.

Two factors drive price away from a Multiple Node on retest:

1. **Position defense.** Traders who accumulated at the node level defend those positions when price returns. They add to or hold their positions, creating directional pressure.

2. **Opposing side closing.** Traders on the wrong side of the node recognize the institutional concentration and close their positions. Their closing orders add fuel to the move away from the node.

More nodes = stronger signal. Two is the minimum. Three or four consecutive HVNs at the same level is a high-conviction setup.

---

## Step-by-Step Rules

1. Set your Order Flow chart to display **Volumes** (preferred) or **Bid x Ask**. Use the 30-minute chart.
2. Look for the yellow highlighted price level that appears when 2 or more consecutive footprint bars have their HVN at the same price. This is your Multiple Node.
3. Confirm the context. The Multiple Node must form in one of three situations:
   - Within a running trend (up or down)
   - Before a trend begins (at the base of a move that then takes off)
   - Within a strong rejection of higher or lower prices
4. Confirm that price has moved **away** from the Multiple Node level. At least 1-2 complete footprint bars must have formed entirely above (if node is support) or below (if node is resistance) the node level.
5. Wait for price to pull back and return to the Multiple Node price level.
6. Enter at the Multiple Node price level.
7. Place your stop loss beyond the node level with a small buffer.
8. **Trade the first touch only.** If price has already tested the node once and bounced, the setup is done. Do not take a second test.

---

## Direction Rules

| Price Approach | Trade Direction |
|---|---|
| Price comes down to the Multiple Node from above | Long |
| Price comes up to the Multiple Node from below | Short |

The direction is determined by which side price is approaching from, not by the trend direction alone. If the node formed in an uptrend and price pulls back down to it, you go Long. If the node formed in a downtrend and price bounces up to it, you go Short.

---

## Two Factors That Drive Price

When price returns to a Multiple Node:

- **Institutional position holders defend.** They transacted heavily at this level across multiple bars. They have skin in the game. When price returns, they defend aggressively.
- **Opposing traders close.** Anyone fighting the institutional position at the node level recognizes the risk and exits. Their closing orders amplify the move away from the node.

The more nodes stacked at the same level, the more institutional interest is concentrated there, and the stronger the expected reaction.

---

## Examples Description

**Trend example:** NQ is in an uptrend on the 30-minute chart. Three consecutive bars all have their HVN highlighted in yellow at 21,050. Price continues up for 2 more bars, then pulls back. When price returns to 21,050, you enter long. The institutional buyers who accumulated across those three bars defend the level, and price bounces back up.

**Pre-trend example:** NQ is consolidating. Two consecutive bars form with HVN at the same level. Then price breaks out strongly upward, forming 2+ bars above the node. Price pulls back to the node level. You enter long. The node was the base of the move, and the same buyers who built positions there defend on the retest.

**Rejection example:** NQ spikes down sharply, then reverses. Within the rejection candles, two bars share an HVN at the same level. Price moves up 2+ bars above the node. Price pulls back to the node. You enter long.

---

## When to Use

- Two or more consecutive footprint bars with HVN at the exact same price level (yellow highlight)
- Context is a trend, pre-trend base, or strong rejection
- At least 1-2 complete footprint bars have formed away from the node before the pullback
- First test of the node after price has moved away

---

## When NOT to Use

- Only one HVN at the level (not a Multiple Node, just a single HVN)
- The node formed in choppy, directionless price action with no clear context
- Price has already tested the node once (first test only rule)
- The node is very old and market structure has shifted significantly
- Price hasn't moved away from the node yet (no confirmation bars above/below)

## NQ/ES-Specific Notes

- NQ Multiple Nodes at round numbers (21,000, 21,050, 21,100) carry extra weight because retail traders also cluster orders there. Institutional + retail defense = stronger reaction.
- On NQ, the 30-minute chart is the primary timeframe. Nodes on the 5-minute chart exist but are less reliable due to lower volume per bar.
- ES Multiple Nodes tend to be more precise because ES has higher liquidity and tighter spreads. NQ nodes may require a slightly wider entry buffer.
- During the first 30 minutes of RTH (9:30-10:00 ET), Multiple Nodes can form quickly as institutions establish positions for the day. These early nodes often become key levels for the rest of the session.
- If a Multiple Node aligns with a prior day's high, low, or settlement price, treat it as a high-conviction setup. The confluence of technical levels and institutional volume concentration makes the reaction more reliable.
- Three or four nodes at the same level on NQ is rare. When it happens, the reaction tends to be fast and significant. Size accordingly and don't miss the entry.
