# Volume-Based Take Profit

## Core Rule

Take profit **before** the next heavy volume area. Heavy volume = potential S/R = price may reverse there. Don't let a winning trade turn into a loser by running into a wall.

## How to Identify Heavy Volume Zones

Use the **30-minute Volume cell content** chart. Look ahead of your position for price levels where the horizontal bars are noticeably thicker than surrounding levels. Those thick bars are where institutions traded heavily — they're the zones that matter.

## Placement Rules

**Place TP slightly before the heavy volume zone, not at it.** Price sometimes reacts a few ticks early as participants anticipate the level. Giving yourself a small buffer (2-5 ticks for NQ) captures most of the move without getting caught in the reversal.

If the closest heavy volume zone is too close to your entry:

- **Option A**: Skip the trade. Bad R:R is bad R:R — no setup justifies a 1:1 or worse.
- **Option B**: Target the next heavy volume zone beyond it. If the first zone is thin enough that price might slice through, the second zone becomes your TP.

## Long vs Short

Works identically for both directions:

- **Long**: TP before the next heavy volume zone above current price
- **Short**: TP before the next heavy volume zone below current price

## Forex vs Futures

This method doesn't require Bid x Ask data. It works on total volume, so it applies to Forex pairs as well as futures. The logic is the same — find where the most volume traded, and don't let price run into it.

## NQ-Specific Notes

For NQ intraday trading, check both the current session and the previous day's session for heavy volume zones. Overnight levels carry over. A zone from yesterday's afternoon session can stop a morning move cold.

Typical workflow:
1. Mark heavy volume zones on the 30-minute chart before the session opens
2. When a trade triggers, scan forward to the next marked zone
3. Set TP 2-5 ticks before that zone
4. If zone is within 10 points of entry, evaluate R:R before taking the trade

## Why This Works

Institutions that traded heavily at a price level have positions anchored there. When price returns, they defend those positions — or opposing participants close out to avoid fighting them. Either way, price tends to stall or reverse. Getting out before that happens is the edge.
