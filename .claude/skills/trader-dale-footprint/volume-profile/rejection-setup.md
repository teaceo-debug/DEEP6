# VP Setup #3: Rejection Setup

The most difficult of the three VP setups. Takes the most practice to identify correctly.

## The Logic

A strong rejection is a moment of decisive institutional aggression. Price moves hard in one direction, then suddenly and aggressively reverses. One side overwhelmed the other.

The Volume Cluster within the rejection marks where the biggest fight happened — the price levels where the most volume traded during the reversal. That's where the winning side was most active, and where the losing side was forced to capitulate.

When price returns to that cluster later, the same participants are still there. The winners defend their positions. The losers, approaching from the other side, close out to avoid fighting them again. Both forces push price away from the zone.

## What a Strong Rejection Looks Like

The key word is **aggressiveness**. You're not looking for a specific candle pattern — you're looking for the character of the move.

A strong rejection has two phases:
1. Price moves aggressively in one direction (fast, with momentum)
2. Price then reverses and moves aggressively in the opposite direction (equally fast, equally committed)

The reversal should be sharp, not gradual. A slow drift back doesn't qualify. You want a clear "V" or inverted "V" shape where the turn is sudden.

Pin bars and reversal candles often show this pattern, but the pattern itself isn't what matters. The aggressiveness of both legs is what matters.

## Step-by-Step Process

**Step 1:** Find a strong rejection on the 30-minute chart. Look for a price level where the market moved hard one way, then reversed hard the other way. The rejection should be visually obvious — it should stand out from the surrounding price action.

**Step 2:** Apply a Flexible Volume Profile to the rejection area. Draw it to cover the entire rejection move, from where the aggressive move started to where the reversal completed.

**Step 3:** Identify where volumes were heaviest within the rejection. This is typically near the turning point — where the most trading happened as the two sides clashed. That cluster is your S/R zone.

**Step 4:** Mark the zone. Note the price range of the heavy volume cluster.

**Step 5:** Wait for price to return to the zone. When it does, open the 5-minute Bid x Ask footprint and look for confirmation before entering.

## Trade Direction

- **Rejection of lower prices** (price fell, then reversed sharply upward) = the zone is **Support**. Trade Long when price returns.
- **Rejection of higher prices** (price rose, then reversed sharply downward) = the zone is **Resistance**. Trade Short when price returns.

The direction of the reversal tells you which side won. The winners' cluster is the level you're trading from.

## Why This Setup Is Harder

**Problem 1: Subjectivity of "strong."** There's no objective threshold for how aggressive a rejection needs to be. With practice you develop a feel for it, but early on it's easy to see rejections everywhere or miss the real ones.

**Problem 2: Messy volume distribution.** In a clean rejection, the volume clusters clearly at the turning point. In a messy one, volume is spread across the entire move with no obvious concentration. When the distribution isn't clean, the setup loses its edge. Skip it.

**Problem 3: Overlap with other setups.** Sometimes a rejection occurs within a rotation (Accumulation Setup) or within a trend (Trend Setup). In those cases, the other setup's logic is stronger. Use the Rejection Setup when the rejection is the dominant feature of the price action, not a secondary element.

## Distinguishing Good from Bad Rejections

**Good rejection:**
- Both legs of the move are fast and committed
- The reversal is sharp, not gradual
- Volume clusters clearly at or near the turning point
- The rejection stands out visually from surrounding price action
- Price hasn't returned to the zone multiple times already (fresh zone)

**Weak rejection:**
- One leg is aggressive, the other is slow
- Volume is spread evenly through the move with no clear cluster
- The rejection blends into surrounding chop
- Price has already tested the zone and it's become messy

## Entry Confirmation

Same principle as the other two setups. The VP zone identifies where to watch. Order flow on the 5-minute Bid x Ask chart confirms when to enter:
- Buying Imbalances at the zone (for longs)
- Selling Imbalances at the zone (for shorts)
- Big limit orders defending the zone
- Delta turning in your favor

Don't enter on price touching the zone alone. The Rejection Setup has more false positives than the other two, so confirmation is especially important here.

## Comparison to the Other Setups

| Setup | Difficulty | Frequency | Reliability |
|-------|-----------|-----------|-------------|
| Accumulation | Low | High | Highest |
| Trend | Medium | Medium | High |
| Rejection | High | Medium | Medium-High (when clean) |

Start with Accumulation setups until you're consistently profitable. Add Trend setups next. Only incorporate Rejection setups once you can reliably distinguish strong rejections from weak ones.
