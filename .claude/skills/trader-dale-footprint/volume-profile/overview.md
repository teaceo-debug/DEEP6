# Volume Profile Overview

## What It Is

Volume Profile is a trading indicator that shows **volume at price** rather than volume at time. Instead of the vertical bars you see at the bottom of a standard chart, Volume Profile draws a horizontal histogram where bar thickness represents how much volume traded at each price level.

Thicker bar = more volume at that price. Thinner bar = less volume.

## Why It's Different from Standard Indicators

Most indicators use two variables: price and time. Volume Profile uses three: price, time, and volume. That third variable is what makes it genuinely predictive rather than just descriptive.

Standard indicators tell you what price did. Volume Profile tells you where institutions actually traded. Those are different questions, and the second one is more useful for anticipating future price behavior.

The 10 largest banks control roughly 65% of market volume. When you track where the most volume traded, you're tracking where those institutions built their positions. Price tends to react at those levels because the participants who created them are still there, defending or closing.

## Profile Shapes

**D-Shape (Most Common)**
A bell curve. Heavy volumes in the middle, thin at the extremes. Indicates a period of balance and rotation where neither buyers nor sellers dominated. Institutions use these periods to build large positions for the next directional move. The heavy volume in the middle becomes strong S/R.

**P-Shape**
Heavy volumes at the top of the profile, thin at the bottom. Aggressive buying pushed price up, then the market rotated at the top as institutions accumulated. Seen during uptrends or at the end of downtrends. The heavy volume cluster at the top is resistance that often becomes support once broken.

**b-Shape**
Mirror of P-Shape. Heavy volumes at the bottom, thin at the top. Aggressive selling pushed price down, then rotation at the bottom. Seen during downtrends or at the end of uptrends. The heavy volume cluster at the bottom is support that often becomes resistance once broken.

**Thin Profile (I-Shape with Bumps)**
A mostly thin, vertical profile with small volume clusters scattered through it. Indicates a strong trend where price moved quickly without much accumulation time. The small bumps are where institutions briefly paused to add to positions. Dale's Trend Setup is built around these bumps.

## Heavy Volume vs Low Volume Zones

**Heavy volume zones:** Price slows down or reverses here. Institutions have positions anchored at these levels. When price returns, they defend (or opposing participants close to avoid fighting them). Either way, price reacts.

**Low volume zones:** Price passes through quickly. No significant positions were built here. No one has a reason to defend these levels. When price enters a low-volume zone, it tends to move fast until it hits the next heavy volume area.

This asymmetry is the foundation of all three VP setups. You're always trading from heavy volume toward the next heavy volume, with low volume in between acting as the highway.

## Timeframe

The 30-minute chart is the primary timeframe for VP analysis. It gives enough resolution to see meaningful volume clusters without the noise of shorter timeframes. The 15-minute and 1-hour charts work as well, but 30-minute is the standard.

## Flexible vs Fixed Profiles

A **Fixed Profile** covers a set time period (e.g., daily, weekly). A **Flexible Profile** lets you draw it over any custom range you define. For the three VP setups, you'll use Flexible Profiles drawn specifically over the rotation, trend segment, or rejection you're analyzing.
