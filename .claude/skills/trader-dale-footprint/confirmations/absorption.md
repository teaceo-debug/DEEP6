# Confirmation #2: Absorption

## Overview

Absorption is the second order flow confirmation used to time entries at pre-identified S/R zones. It's conceptually similar to the Big Limit Orders confirmation but easier to spot: instead of looking for one large number on one side, you're looking for heavy volume on **both** sides simultaneously at the zone.

Heavy volume on both sides means one group is absorbing everything the other group throws at it. The momentum side is running out of steam. The absorbing side is winning.

This is **not a standalone strategy**. Identify your S/R zone first (via Volume Profile, POC, HVN, or an order flow setup), then look for absorption when price arrives.

---

## When to Use Confirmations

Use this confirmation when any of the following apply:

- Price has reached a VP-based S/R zone and you want timing confirmation before entering
- You're uncertain about the setup and want additional evidence
- Price moved aggressively against your level (momentum is strong, you need proof it's stalling)
- The level isn't particularly strong on its own and needs extra validation

S/R identification always comes first. Absorption at a random price level means nothing.

---

## Step-by-Step Rules

**Short (Resistance) Setup:**

1. Identify resistance zone using Volume Profile
2. Wait for price to push up into that zone (buyers are aggressive, momentum is upward)
3. Open a 5-minute or 30-minute Bid x Ask footprint chart
4. At the resistance zone, look for cells with heavy volume on **both** the Bid AND Ask columns
5. This means sellers are absorbing all the buying pressure — buyers can't push through
6. Confirm that price is stalling or starting to reverse at the zone
7. Enter short when absorption is clearly visible

**Long (Support) Setup:**

1. Identify support zone using Volume Profile
2. Wait for price to push down into that zone (sellers are aggressive, momentum is downward)
3. Open a 5-minute or 30-minute Bid x Ask footprint chart
4. At the support zone, look for cells with heavy volume on **both** the Bid AND Ask columns
5. This means buyers are absorbing all the selling pressure — sellers can't push through
6. Confirm that price is stalling or starting to reverse at the zone
7. Enter long when absorption is clearly visible

---

## What to Look For

**The core pattern:**

Both the Bid and Ask columns at the S/R zone show volumes significantly above the recent session average. Not just one side — both. This is what distinguishes absorption from a simple large limit order.

The logic: if sellers are pushing price down aggressively (lots of Bid volume), but buyers at support are matching every sell with a buy (lots of Ask volume too), the selling momentum is being absorbed. The buyers aren't running. They're standing their ground and taking every contract offered.

**What counts as "unusually large":**

Compare to recent average cell volumes for that instrument and session. Both sides need to be elevated, not just one. A cell where Bid = 150 and Ask = 180 when the session average is 40 per side is a clear absorption signal. A cell where Bid = 150 and Ask = 20 is just aggressive selling, not absorption.

**Timing:**

Large volume doesn't appear all at once. Absorption builds over several minutes as the battle plays out. Don't rush the entry. Watch the zone for 2-5 minutes to confirm the pattern is developing. A single bar with heavy volume on both sides is a start; multiple bars showing the same pattern at the same price zone is confirmation.

**Where it appears:**

Absorption can show up in a single cell (one price level with heavy two-sided volume) or spread across 2-3 adjacent cells within the zone. Both are valid. The zone itself is what matters, not the exact price tick.

---

## Timeframe

**Primary:** 5-minute Bid x Ask footprint chart  
**Secondary:** 30-minute Bid x Ask footprint chart

The 5-minute is the default. Use the 30-minute when you want a higher-level view of the absorption pattern, especially at major S/R zones where the battle may play out over a longer period. The 30-minute can confirm what the 5-minute is showing, or catch absorption that's too spread out to read clearly on the shorter timeframe.

Don't use 1-minute for absorption. The bars are too short to accumulate meaningful two-sided volume, and you'll get too many false reads.

---

## Combo With Other Confirmations

Absorption pairs naturally with Confirmation #3 (Aggressive Orders and Delta).

The sequence to watch for:
1. Absorption appears at the S/R zone (heavy two-sided volume, momentum stalling)
2. Aggressive orders then pile in from participants who recognize the reversal
3. Delta shifts in the direction of the trade

This is the same snowball dynamic as with Big Limit Orders. The absorption is the anchor; the aggressive orders that follow are the confirmation that the reversal is real and other participants are joining.

Absorption and Big Limit Orders (Confirmation #1) can appear together. A large limit order on one side plus heavy volume on the other side = the limit order is being absorbed. Both signals reinforce each other.

---

## NQ/ES Notes

**NQ (Nasdaq futures):**
- NQ moves fast. Absorption can develop and resolve in 2-3 minutes at a level. Don't wait too long or the move is already underway.
- Because NQ is thinner, absorption signals can be more dramatic when they appear. A 5x spike in two-sided volume at a level is not unusual.
- Watch for absorption at round numbers (21000, 21500, etc.) and prior day high/low. These are the levels where big players tend to defend positions.

**ES (S&P 500 futures):**
- ES absorption tends to be more gradual. The deeper book means more contracts need to change hands before the pattern becomes obvious.
- On ES, absorption at major levels (prior day high, weekly open, key VP nodes) is more reliable than at minor intraday levels.
- Both instruments: the 30-minute chart is more useful on ES than NQ because ES absorption often plays out over a longer time window.

**General:**
- Absorption is most reliable during the first 2 hours of regular session (9:30-11:30 ET) and the last hour (3:00-4:00 ET). Midday absorption signals have more noise and lower follow-through.
- Always confirm the S/R zone is meaningful before trusting the absorption signal. Absorption at a weak level is less reliable than absorption at a high-volume node or major structural level.
