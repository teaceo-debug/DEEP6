# Volume Profile Shapes — Distribution Pattern Recognition

Profile shape is the first thing you read before anything else. It tells you what the market was doing all day in a single glance: building value, trending, rotating, or rejecting. Shape recognition is the foundation of every LVN trade because the shape determines where LVNs form, how significant they are, and what the directional bias is.

---

## The 5 Core Profile Shapes

### 1. Normal / Bell (D-Shape)

The classic bell curve. Volume clusters symmetrically around a central POC, tapering off toward both extremes. About 70% of sessions produce some version of this shape.

**What it means:** The market found fair value and spent the day building consensus around it. Neither buyers nor sellers dominated. Participants were comfortable transacting near the POC.

**LVN structure:** LVNs appear at both tails, between the POC and the session extremes. These are thin because price moved quickly through those levels to reach the extremes, then returned. They're real but not particularly high-conviction.

**Directional bias:** None. This is a balanced session. The market is in equilibrium.

**Strategy:** Mean reversion within the Value Area. Fade extremes back toward POC. Avoid breakout trades unless the next session opens outside the VA.

---

### 2. P-Shape (Bullish Migration)

POC sits at the bottom of the profile. Price extended upward, leaving a long thin tail above the main volume cluster.

**What it means:** Buyers absorbed all available supply at lower prices and pushed the market up. Most volume transacted at the lower prices (accumulation zone), but the market closed near the highs. Value is migrating upward.

**LVN structure:** The tail above the POC is the LVN zone. Price moved through those upper levels quickly, leaving thin participation. These LVNs are high-quality because they formed during a directional move with clear intent.

**Directional bias:** Bullish. The market is telling you buyers are in control and value is moving higher.

**Strategy:** Long bias on pullbacks to the lower HVN (the main volume cluster). The LVN in the tail acts as a highway for continuation. If price re-enters the tail, it should move quickly back toward the highs or break down entirely.

---

### 3. b-Shape (Bearish Migration)

POC sits at the top of the profile. Price extended downward, leaving a long thin tail below the main volume cluster.

**What it means:** Sellers distributed at higher prices and pushed the market down. Most volume transacted at the upper prices (distribution zone), but the market closed near the lows. Value is migrating downward.

**LVN structure:** The tail below the POC is the LVN zone. Same logic as P-shape but inverted. These LVNs formed during a directional sell-off.

**Directional bias:** Bearish. Sellers are in control and value is moving lower.

**Strategy:** Short bias on rallies to the upper HVN. The LVN in the lower tail acts as a highway for continuation lower. If price re-enters the tail from below, expect fast movement back toward the lows or a full reversal.

---

### 4. B-Shape (Bimodal / Double Distribution)

Two distinct volume peaks with a clear LVN valley between them. The profile looks like two stacked bell curves.

**What it means:** The market rotated between two distinct value areas during the session. It built consensus at one level, then auctioned to a new level and built consensus there too. This is a rotation day.

**LVN structure:** The LVN between the two peaks is the most critical level of the session. It's the decision zone where the market transitioned from one value area to the other. This LVN is often structural and persists into future sessions.

**Directional bias:** Ambiguous during formation, but the closing location matters. If price closes in the upper distribution, bias is bullish. If it closes in the lower distribution, bias is bearish.

**Strategy:** Two approaches. Range trading: fade the extremes of each distribution back toward their respective POCs. Breakout trade: if one distribution wins decisively (price leaves and doesn't return), trade the continuation through the LVN toward the next target.

The LVN between peaks is the key. A clean break and hold above it is bullish. A rejection back below it is bearish.

---

### 5. Thin / Elongated (Trending Day)

No clear POC. Volume is spread relatively evenly across a wide price range. The profile looks like a thin rectangle or a slightly tapered column.

**What it means:** The market trended all day without pausing to build value. Participants kept transacting at progressively higher (or lower) prices without consolidating. This is the rarest shape.

**LVN structure:** Almost the entire profile is an LVN relative to a normal session. There are no meaningful HVNs to anchor to. The profile itself is the highway.

**Directional bias:** Strong. The direction of the trend is obvious from the price action. The profile confirms it.

**Strategy:** Trend following only. Counter-trend trades have no structural support. There are no HVNs to bounce from, no POC to mean-revert to. Ride the trend and use prior session levels for context.

## LVN Relationship by Shape

| Shape | LVN Location | LVN Quality | Count |
|-------|-------------|-------------|-------|
| Normal (D) | Both tails | Moderate | 2-4 |
| P-Shape | Upper tail above POC | High | 1-3 |
| b-Shape | Lower tail below POC | High | 1-3 |
| B-Shape | Between the two peaks | Very High | 1 critical |
| Thin | Entire profile | Low (no contrast) | N/A |

The B-shape LVN between peaks is the highest-quality single LVN you'll encounter. It formed during a real auction transition, not just a fast move through thin air.

## Reading the Developing Profile

Shape identification isn't just for end-of-day analysis. You can read the developing profile in real time to anticipate what kind of session you're in.

**First 30-60 minutes (Initial Balance):** The IB establishes the first HVN. If the IB is narrow and volume is concentrated, you're likely heading toward a D-shape or B-shape. If the IB is wide and volume is spread, you may be in a trending day.

**Shape transitions:** A session can start as a D-shape and transition to a P or b-shape if a directional move develops after the open. Watch for the POC to shift. If the POC starts moving consistently in one direction, the shape is transitioning.

**Early clues:**
- Narrow IB + balanced volume = D-shape likely
- Wide IB + one-sided volume = P or b-shape developing
- Price leaving IB and not returning = trending day or B-shape forming
- Price leaving IB, returning, then leaving again to the other side = B-shape

## NQ-Specific Observations

NQ has a strong tendency toward P and b-shapes on trend days. The index is driven by a handful of mega-cap names, and when those names move, NQ moves fast and far. The resulting profiles are often asymmetric with clear directional tails.

B-shapes are common on rotation days, particularly around macro events (FOMC, CPI, earnings). The market tests one extreme, rejects, tests the other, and often closes somewhere in the middle. The LVN between the two distributions on these days is frequently tested in the following session.

D-shapes dominate on low-volatility days and during consolidation phases. They're the least useful for LVN trading because the LVNs are weak and the mean-reversion tendency is strong.

Thin/elongated profiles are rare in NQ but occur on major trend days (large macro surprises, index rebalancing). When you see one, don't fight it.
